## Summary

<div class="card">
  <p>I am a collaborative PhD biostatistician and a Research Scientist in Research and Program Evaluation at the New York State Office of Addiction Services and Supports (OASAS). At OASAS, I apply quantitative and geospatial analysis to evaluate statewide addiction services and workforce programs. My doctoral research and broader work as a biostatistician center on Bayesian hierarchical spatial modeling, observational studies, and building HIPAA-compliant geospatial pipelines and R packages for multidisciplinary clinical and public health teams. Across both my applied evaluation work and methodological research, I focus on translating complex statistical methods into clear, actionable insights for clinical, program, and policy audiences.</p>
</div>

### New York University Grossman School of Medicine

<div class="cv-entry">
  <div class="cv-entry__title">Graduate Research Assistant</div>
  <div class="cv-entry__meta">2021–2026</div>
  <p>Advisors: Samrachana Adhikari, PhD; Keith Goldfeld, PhD</p>

  <p><strong>Dissertation Aim 1 — Urban pharmacy accessibility</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li>First-author manuscript accepted for publication in <em>Spatial and Spatio-temporal Epidemiology</em> (June 2026).</li>
    <li>Developed a transportation mode-adjusted two-step floating catchment area (2SFCA) framework for inferential transparency in survey-based spatial analyses of pharmacy access. Used a hierarchical Monte Carlo simulation to propagate American Community Survey sampling uncertainty through access estimates.</li>
    <li>Produced geospatial plots, road-network routing outputs, and cross-tabulations characterizing access-classification sensitivity; led statistical analyses.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>Dissertation Aim 2 — Bayesian spatial modeling and simulation</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li>Developed functionality for penalized complexity priors in a full Bayesian framework in Stan (manuscript in writing).</li>
    <li>Conducted a simulation study comparing areal spatial model specifications under varying hyperprior assumptions.</li>
    <li>Demonstrated an importance-sampling approach for a spatial model to reduce time-consuming model refits.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>Collaborative research contributions</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li>Designed HIPAA-compliant geospatial pipelines on HPC infrastructure (Singularity) for large-scale EHR and Census datasets, including cross-institution use.</li>
    <li>Produced geospatial visualizations and conducted statistical analyses supporting a multi-disciplinary team studying medication adherence in a heart failure population of over 6,000 patients; the team found nonadherence to guideline-directed therapy ranged from 40% in the highest-socioeconomic-status neighborhoods to 52% in the lowest, with 35–57% higher adjusted odds of nonadherence in lower-SES areas (<em>JAMA Network Open</em>, 2023).</li>
    <li>Contributed to a follow-on machine-learning effort predicting continuous medication-adherence scores from over 120 patient-, therapy-, and neighborhood-level predictors; a superlearner ensemble achieved the lowest prediction error among several ML approaches, outperforming traditional regression (<em>JAMIA</em>, 2025), with a companion paper examining algorithmic fairness of the resulting model (<em>JAMIA</em>, 2026).</li>
    <li>Conducted statistical analysis for a co-authored paper monitoring off-label ketamine clinic advertising in the NYC metro area via a systematic web search (<em>Fundamental &amp; Clinical Pharmacology</em>, 2025).</li>
  </ul>
</div>

### Additional experience (intermittent, 2018–present)

<div class="cv-entry">
  <div class="cv-entry__title">Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai</div>

  <p><strong>Center for Scientific Workforce Excellence and Advancement</strong> (CSWEA) — 2018–present<br>
  Graduate Research Assistant in Statistics and Data Science. Mentor: Emma K. T. Benn, DrPH (Founding Director).</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Since 2018, collaborated intermittently on disparities-analytics and workforce-diversity research with CSWEA, including REDCap program evaluation, discrimination-related outcomes modeling, Google Trends surveillance, and methods simulation; contributed choropleth visualizations of statewide opioid-abuse and poverty associations for the 2019 ASA "This Is Statistics" Data Challenge.</li>
    <li>Collaborated with CSWEA colleagues on a team entry in the STAI-X 2026 Data Challenge (Harvard University Departments of Statistics and Biostatistics, with the ASA StatsUpAI Interest Group), building a statistical/AI pipeline to forecast state-level rates of suspected nonfatal opioid-overdose emergency department visits from social, environmental, behavioral, and clinical-surveillance signals.</li>
    <li>Consulted with Dr. Karen Hicklin on the appropriate statistical approach to summarize NIH K99/R00 and MOSAIC awardee career-transition data (a merged NIH Data Book cohort of roughly 4,600 awardees); proposed defining "time to R01" by award-sequence transition step rather than calendar time, for a manuscript examining NIH's return on early-career investment in underrepresented scientists.</li>
    <li>Contributed statistical programming for BMI- and age-adjusted uterine cancer incidence rate estimates by state and histologic subtype, supporting an ongoing project led by Dr. Tracy Layne.</li>
    <li>Provided ad hoc statistical consultation on variable-selection methods for a Mount Sinai faculty collaborator, recommending regularization-based approaches (e.g., elastic net) as an alternative to stepwise selection.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>PAIRED Lab</strong> (Pain, Autonomics and Immune Research in Diverse Populations), Department of Neurology — 2021–2026<br>
  Statistical Consultant. PI: Jessica Robinson-Papp, MD.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Collaborated on the EVA/CANDLE study (R01DK122853) as statistical consultant, applying Fourier analysis and power spectral decomposition to characterize GI motility-capsule pressure rhythmicity in HIV-associated autonomic neuropathy; contributed statistical framing to the study's 2024 NIH competing renewal.</li>
    <li>First author, <em>Journal of Neuroimmune Pharmacology</em> (2023): network analysis of 44 immune markers across 42 adults with HIV found significantly greater cytokine-network complexity — stronger, more numerous positive correlations among immune markers — with increasing autonomic neuropathy severity (preprint: Research Square, 2023).</li>
    <li>Built and maintain <em>tidyRHRV</em> (CRAN/R-universe), an R package for reading, filtering, and analyzing heart-rate-variability data across multiple ECG datasets at once, supporting a line of autonomic/HRV work including a 2022 paper on interpreting resting HRV in complex populations (<em>Clinical Autonomic Research</em>) and first-author work on ECG data cleaning (<em>J Neurol Sci</em>, 2021) and PCA-based autonomic neuropathy phenotyping (<em>Autonomic Neuroscience</em>, 2023).</li>
    <li>Co-author on related autonomic-neuropathy publications in <em>Neurology: Clinical Practice</em> (2023) and <em>AIDS Care</em> (2024).</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>Departments of Psychiatry and Emergency Medicine</strong> — 2024</p>
